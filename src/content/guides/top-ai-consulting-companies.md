## In one sentence

**AI consulting is four different businesses competing on one search term, and picking correctly means identifying which of the four your problem belongs to before you take a call.**

Search "AI consulting companies" and the first page mixes a global professional services firm
with a fifty-person development shop and a two-person consultancy. All three are legitimate.
They are not substitutes, and the difference is not quality. It is what they are built to sell.

This page maps the category so you can tell them apart. We build automation for inbound
operations, so we belong to one of the four types described below, and we have said which one.

## The four types, and what each is actually built to sell

### Global consultancies

Accenture, EY, IBM Consulting, BCG, Deloitte, PwC. They appear at the top of this search
because they publish extensively and carry enormous domain authority, and because they do
genuinely run large AI programmes.

What they are built to sell is a programme: multiple departments, procurement, governance,
risk and compliance review, change management, and a delivery arm to implement afterwards.
The engagement model assumes a budget cycle and an executive sponsor.

They are the correct choice when the decision spans functions, when regulatory review is part
of the scope, or when the organisation needs an external name to unblock an internal argument.
That last reason is unfashionable to state and it is frequently the real one.

They are the wrong choice when the problem is one process. The overhead that makes them
effective on a cross-functional programme is pure cost on a routing rule.

### Development companies

Firms whose primary business is building software, with consulting as the front end of a build.
LeewayHertz and Appinventiv are visible examples in this search, and the pattern is easy to
recognise: the site carries pages for agent development, app development, and hiring engineers
alongside the consulting page.

What they sell is delivery capacity. The consulting phase exists to scope a build, and the
economics work when a build follows.

They are the correct choice when you have decided what to build and need people to build it,
particularly when the thing is genuinely custom. They are a poor choice when the honest answer
is a configuration change in software you already own, because that answer does not fit their
model.

This is not a criticism. It is a structural incentive, and you should read every recommendation
with it in mind, exactly as you should read ours.

### Specialist automation agencies

Smaller firms working one vertical or one problem class. In this search that includes
automation agencies focused on marketing operations, revenue operations, or inbound lead flow.

What they sell is depth in a narrow area. The engagement is weeks rather than quarters, the
scope is one process, and the person you meet is usually the person who does the work.

They are the correct choice when your problem is bounded and you can name it. They are the
wrong choice when you do not yet know which process is broken, because a specialist will find
work in their speciality.

### Independent consultants

One person, deep experience, no delivery arm. Frequently the best value in the category and the
hardest to find, because a single consultant cannot outrank a firm with a content team.

They are the correct choice when you need judgement rather than hands, and when the decision is
genuinely a decision rather than a build. The constraint is capacity and the bus factor.

## The question that sorts the four

Ask yourself what would happen if the assessment concluded that you should do nothing.

If that outcome is imaginable and acceptable, you want advice, and the last two types fit. If
it is not, because the budget exists to build something and the decision has effectively been
made, you want delivery, and the first two fit.

Teams that get this wrong buy advice when they wanted hands, then complain that the deliverable
was a document.

## What a good engagement produces

Regardless of type, the artefacts are the same and you can ask for them in advance.

A current-state map with measured numbers rather than estimates. On inbound work that means
four reconciled counts: requests that arrived, records created, records with a live owner, and
records actually worked. The method is in the [inbound lead audit](/guides/inbound-lead-audit/),
and you can run it yourself before hiring anyone.

An opportunity list ranked by the cost of the current failure. Not by effort, not by how
interesting the technology is. The most expensive defect in a typical inbound funnel is a
routing rule with no fallback branch, which is dull and cheap to fix.

A build, buy, or configure decision per opportunity, with the reasoning written down so someone
who was not in the room can disagree with it.

A sequence with an exit test per step. Without exit tests every release is judged on whether it
felt better, and programmes continue past the point where they stopped adding value.

## Six warning signs

**A maturity model with no process map.** Scoring your organisation from one to five is a sales
artefact. It creates a gap and positions the firm to fill it. Ask what specific handoff is
failing and how often.

**Every recommendation needs their implementation arm.** If the assessment produces no item you
could do yourself, the assessment was scoped to produce a build.

**No exit test.** If nobody agrees in advance what number decides whether step one worked, step
two will happen regardless.

**Undisclosed reseller relationships.** A recommendation that pays the recommender is not
advice. Ask directly, in writing, and read the answer carefully.

**Nothing in the "do not do" column.** Any honest assessment of a real business contains
recommendations against action. A document where everything is worth doing was written to be
agreed with.

**A price before seeing data.** Scoping without seeing the funnel is guesswork, and guesswork
gets priced defensively. Firms that quote early are charging you for their uncertainty.

## How to run the shortlist

Pick one firm from the type your problem belongs to, and one from a neighbouring type as a
control. Give both the same brief and the same data.

Ask each for the four counts and see who asks for the export. Ask each what they would
recommend against. Ask each what happens if the assessment says do nothing.

The answers separate the category faster than any comparison table, including this one.

## The uncomfortable conclusion

For a large share of teams searching this term, the correct answer is not to hire an AI
consulting company at all. It is to run the reconciliation yourself, find the handoff with no
fallback branch, and fix it.

That takes an afternoon and no budget. If it turns out the funnel is sound and the problem is
demand, no consultancy of any type changes that arithmetic, and knowing it early is worth more
than a maturity score.
