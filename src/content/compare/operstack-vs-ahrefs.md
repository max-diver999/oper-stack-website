## What are OperStack and Ahrefs for?

OperStack Watch combines 10 weekly ChatGPT questions, a sample of your pages and written fixes in one email for $35 a month. Ahrefs sells SEO research and several AI products. Its standalone Custom Prompts package starts at $50; Lite includes a smaller prompt allowance alongside the SEO suite.

1. **It reads your pages, not only your name.** The free Ahrefs AI Visibility Checker asks for a brand name and reads none of your pages. The OperStack [free check](/ai-visibility/) asks for your address, reads up to ten pages sampled from your sitemap, and scores each one on five groups of checks for readable, attributable content. Watch re-reads ten pages every week. Ahrefs Site Audit crawls far more pages, but it looks for SEO issues, and the Ahrefs pages we read do not describe it checking whether a page opens with an answer an AI system could lift.

2. **It writes the paragraph for you.** If a page it reads lacks a short opening answer, the free check drafts one from what your own pages say, with a "Copy the text" button under it. Watch selects one priority fix from the sampled pages each week, with the page address and proposed words to review. The writing help Ahrefs sells for existing pages is titles and meta descriptions, through Ask AI in Project Boost Pro at $20 a month per project. Ready answer paragraphs are not stated on its pages.

3. **It names who ChatGPT recommended instead, even for free.** The free check asks ChatGPT three buyer questions with web search on and lists the names it gave, sorted into real competitors, non-competitors such as portals and law firms, and names it could not identify. The free Ahrefs checker has no competitor comparison; Ahrefs keeps that for paid Brand Radar.

4. **It sends one email a week with what changed.** Every 7 days from the day you subscribe, Watch emails whether ChatGPT named you for each of ten questions, who it named in your place, how three rivals measure up beside you and what moved since the last letter. Ahrefs emails Rank Tracker reports weekly or monthly; a weekly Brand Radar email is not stated on the pages we read.

5. **Its free ChatGPT questions are asked live.** Each free check puts three questions to ChatGPT at the moment you run it, allows up to three such runs per connection per day, and shows the saved answer if you repeat the same site within 24 hours. The Ahrefs checker shows a top-five preview drawn from its index, and its page does not make clear whether each lookup runs live.

6. **Monitoring and page work share one subscription.** Watch is $35 a month or $299 a year on our [pricing page](/pricing/). Ahrefs Custom Prompts starts at $50 monthly for 2,500 checks; Lite includes 5 prompts with its SEO suite for $129, and the index costs $199 a month for one AI platform or $699 for all of them. The index is optional and is not required to buy Custom Prompts.

7. **It needs no developer and no SEO skills.** Text fixes arrive with wording to review and paste; where a change needs a developer, the letter flags it. It is written for the person who owns the business, not for a specialist reading a dashboard.

8. **You can try it and leave.** The first 7 days of Watch are free and you can cancel any time. Ahrefs has no free trial, and its pricing page says it does not issue refunds in general.

### What is Ahrefs for, and what is OperStack for?

Ahrefs is an SEO data platform with AI tracking added as a paid module; OperStack is an AI visibility tool that does one job.

### OperStack, in plain words

OperStack has two parts: the free check described above, and Watch, which turns it into a weekly letter with ten questions, three rivals and the priority page fix. It holds no keyword or backlink data. Our [Semrush comparison](/compare/operstack-vs-semrush/) looks at the other large suite in the same terms.

Four details change the real Ahrefs bill.

**Annual billing saves up to 17%.** Lite drops from $1,548 over twelve monthly payments to $1,290 paid once, and the switch goes through Ahrefs support. Watch for a year is $299, less than a quarter of that.

### Is Ahrefs Brand Radar worth it for a small business?

<!-- cite-block:start -->
For a local business, check whether the index covers the actual questions that matter. Ahrefs itself warns that the index "may have limited coverage for brands with little or no search volume", and the custom prompts that would fill that gap are capped at 5 on Lite. A $35 weekly check on ChatGPT, with the fix written out, is the more direct route for most owners.

The reason lies in how the index is built. Brand Radar draws its prompts from Ahrefs keyword data: 449 million of them according to the Brand Radar page, while the pricing page says 475 million, and we give both because the two pages disagree. The prompts are re-tested monthly over a 90-day window. A family law office serving one county may turn up in none of them, because its category is never typed into Google often enough to become an index prompt. A dental clinic in one suburb may be in the same position.
<!-- cite-block:end -->

Custom prompts are the workaround, and on Lite the arithmetic is tight. Ahrefs counts one prompt on one platform in one location as one check. Five prompts run daily on ChatGPT use the whole 150-check allowance, so adding a second platform means cutting prompts or buying a pack. One Claude update costs 8 checks. Lite bundles SEO tools, so dividing its entire price by 150 checks would misrepresent the cost of its AI feature. A buyer who only needs selected questions can consider the separate $50 Custom Prompts package. Page review and publication remain separate tasks.

A quick test before paying: type your brand into the free Ahrefs checker, which runs on the same index. A sparse result suggests checking coverage before buying the index; custom prompts are a different product and can investigate a brand with little indexed history. Then run the OperStack [free check](/ai-visibility/) on your address: three buyer questions asked the way your buyers ask them, answered live by ChatGPT, with the names it gave in your place.

## How does each tool check AI visibility?

OperStack connects a record of 10 ChatGPT answers with checks of up to 10 pages and suggestions for improving them. Ahrefs Custom Prompts records questions you select, while its separate Brand Radar index searches a stored collection. A page score identifies measurable weaknesses; it cannot establish why an assistant chose a particular business.

| | Ahrefs Brand Radar | OperStack Watch |
|---|---|---|
| What you enter | A brand name | A site address |
| Questions | Index prompts built from keyword data; custom prompts from 5 (Lite) | 10 buyer questions, unchanged week to week |
| How often | Index re-tested monthly; custom prompts daily, weekly or monthly | Every 7 days from your subscription date |
| Competitors | Share of voice against the competitors you track | Names ChatGPT gave instead of you, plus 3 rivals measured beside you |
| Your own pages | Pages that AI answers cite are listed | 10 pages scored on five areas every week |
| What you act on | Mentions, cited domains and cited pages | A page address and a paragraph to paste |

The OperStack score is the layer that turns a mention count into a to-do list. It is built from five areas with published weights, so you can add it up by hand. Access, 25 points, comes from the robots.txt rules for 14 AI crawlers and fetchers. A map for agents, 15 points, comes from llms.txt. Entity, 20 points, comes from business schema, FAQ markup, page type, canonical tags and Open Graph. Quotable content, 25 points, asks for 300 or more words, an answer-first paragraph with a figure right after the H1, at least three H2 sections and a table. Dates and sources, 15 points, looks for dates in meta or schema, named sources and sitemap dates. Brand Radar tells you whether you are mentioned. The score supplies page findings, and Watch proposes edits for review.

A note on Ahrefs' own pages, because they disagree with each other. A help-center article, "What is Ahrefs", lists ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Grok and mentions seven AI indexes, while the Brand Radar page has no Grok. The free checker's introduction names five platforms and its FAQ adds AI Mode as a sixth. For the table above we used the Brand Radar page.

Who writes the prompts differs too. In Ahrefs you type them or let AI generate them, then decide which to keep. In Watch the ten questions are the kind your buyers ask, and they stay fixed, so a change between weeks reflects the answer, not a new question.

## What each tool tells a business owner on a Monday morning

An OperStack subscriber receives 1 email per week with named businesses, page findings and draft fixes. An Ahrefs user can receive SEO reports and open Brand Radar for AI results. Your useful next step is the same in either case: decide which finding warrants an edit, then record what you changed.

<!-- cite-block:start -->
OperStack Watch turns the same 10 weekly questions into a record a business can keep reviewing. Each report identifies the businesses named in those answers, checks up to 10 of your pages and measures 3 rivals beside you. Later emails compare the new observation with the previous week, so the owner can follow a particular question across reports instead of relying on a remembered screenshot. The page findings and proposed wording make the record actionable, while the editor retains control of what gets published. For an Ahrefs user, assign that record to a concrete decision: which commercially relevant page deserves attention this week? Keep keyword and backlink research available where it supports that decision. A rising readiness score indicates that measured page features changed; a rising mention count indicates that more sampled answers named the business. Neither observation, alone, proves that the edit produced more sales.
<!-- cite-block:end -->

1. Assign one commercially useful page finding to its editor.
2. Approve the proposed wording against a current source on your site.
3. Keep the publication date with subsequent question results.

## When you need a different kind of tool

<!-- cite-block:start -->
Ahrefs serves an SEO team working with keyword demand, backlinks and large crawls. Lite includes 750 tracked keywords and 100,000 monthly crawl credits according to its September 2026 pricing page. Those requirements extend beyond Watch's weekly ChatGPT report and sampled page edits, and they can justify a separate research budget.

1. Keep keyword research where you can inspect search demand and the market being measured. OperStack does not supply keyword volumes.
2. Use a backlink index for outreach or link investigation. A list of firms named in ChatGPT answers is a different dataset.
3. Plan a full crawl for a large catalogue or migration. Watch's page sample cannot clear every URL for release.

## Which one fits a clinic, an agency, a SaaS company or an online shop?

A clinic, a 10-client agency, a software company and a shop need different coverage. Watch fits a small set of ChatGPT questions and sampled page edits. A buyer needing daily multi-engine tracking should account for that difference; keyword research and a large crawl require additional tools whatever subscription they choose.

1. **A dental clinic or a small law office**

The owner has no time for dashboards, and the brand has little search volume, which is exactly where Ahrefs says its index is thin. Watch asks ChatGPT the questions patients or clients ask, names the practices it recommends instead, and returns text to paste on the service pages. If an agency already handles the clinic's rankings, that stays a separate job; Watch adds the ChatGPT side without anyone learning a new tool.

2. **An agency with ten clients**

Ahrefs Standard, at $249 a month, includes 10 tracked AI prompts across its 20 projects: one prompt per client if you have ten. Watch gives each client ten questions every week, the named rivals and a priority page fix the agency can deliver as finished work, and ten subscriptions come to $350 a month (our arithmetic; there is no agency tier on our [pricing page](/pricing/) as of 26 September 2026). For pitches, the [MCP server](/mcp/) is free with no account, and one of its five tools lines up between two and four sites on one 100-point scale, from inside Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor.

3. **A SaaS company**

Software buyers ask ChatGPT for alternatives and shortlists, and Watch shows each week which products it names instead of yours, then points to the page and paragraph that could change the answer. Keyword research for a content calendar is a different task and sits with a suite.

4. **An online shop**

Watch answers a narrow but valuable question for a shop: whether ChatGPT names the store when buyers ask where to get a product type, and who it names instead. Keyword data for thousands of category and product pages, and a crawl of the whole catalog, are SEO work for a suite.

## Using Ahrefs and OperStack together, or switching

Keeping Ahrefs for keyword, backlink and crawl work can make sense alongside Watch at $35 monthly. Before dropping any AI package, record the questions, engines and history you still need. Watch covers ChatGPT weekly, so it does not reproduce every Brand Radar dataset or monitoring schedule during a switch.

**What to keep in Ahrefs:** Site Explorer, Keywords Explorer, Rank Tracker and Site Audit. Nothing in OperStack replaces them.

**What to question:** a Brand Radar add-on bought mainly to watch ChatGPT. The index is optional and is not required to buy Custom Prompts.

**What OperStack adds:** ten pages read and scored every week, the rivals named, and the paragraph to paste. Ahrefs' Patches rewrite titles and meta descriptions; Watch writes the opening answers and other on-page text that AI systems quote. Watch asks ChatGPT only: it reads whether the crawlers of other AI engines can reach your site, but it does not track their answers week by week.

**How to move without a gap:**

1. Run the free check on your site. It costs nothing and shows three live ChatGPT answers.
2. Start Watch. Within the first 7 days you pay nothing.
3. If you drop the Brand Radar add-on or a plan, cancel from Account Settings. The plan runs to the end of the paid period, then the account becomes Ahrefs Free, which keeps Site Explorer and Site Audit for verified sites with 5,000 crawl credits a month per project. Ahrefs does not issue refunds in general, so time the cancellation to the end of a billing period.

Both MCP servers can sit side by side in Claude or Cursor. The Ahrefs one needs a paid plan and, for Brand Radar data, the add-on; ours needs no account and no plan. For Google's own free search data, see how OperStack compares with [Google Search Console](/compare/operstack-vs-google-search-console/).

## What our data says about answer-first pages

The OperStack study recorded 214 of 496 scored websites without a qualifying opening answer on any sampled page, approximately 43%. That finding points to a page-writing task an owner can inspect directly. It does not measure Ahrefs performance or prove that adding an opening paragraph changes ChatGPT recommendations.

Source: our free checker, 25 September 2026, examining small-business websites in law, property and dental services across the United States, United Kingdom, Australia and Canada. Pages were sampled; the result does not mean every page on every affected domain was inspected.

| Recorded opening-answer finding | Websites | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| No qualifying opening in the sample | 214 | Review the first substantive paragraph |
| At least one qualifying opening | 282 | Other pages may still need work |
| Scored sample | 496 | Not a random sample of all businesses |

The second row is calculated as 496 minus 214. Qualification is a rule in our checker, not an external certification of writing quality. A paragraph can pass a length-and-number check while giving a poor answer, and a useful paragraph can fall outside a mechanical threshold.

An operator can use the finding by opening a service page, reading only its heading and first paragraph, and checking whether those lines actually answer the customer's question. Include a number only when it belongs in that answer and has a source. Do not add a price, deadline or percentage merely to gain points.

Ahrefs' research tools and a page-writing task therefore belong in different parts of the review. Our [cases page](/cases/) explains the use of our own sites as examples. After an edit, save the old wording and observe the same questions again; a changed mention count is a follow-up observation, not proof of cause.
