## Where does OperStack do more than Ahrefs for AI visibility?

For the one question of whether ChatGPT recommends your business, OperStack gives a small business more of what it needs for less money: it reads your pages, names the rivals ChatGPT chose, writes the fix and emails it every week for $35 a month. In Ahrefs, tracked AI prompts start with Lite at $129 a month and the Brand Radar AI index costs from $199 a month on top. OperStack is our product, so every Ahrefs figure on this page comes from Ahrefs' own pages as they stood on 26 September 2026, with the source linked in the table above.

**1. It reads your pages, not only your name.** The free Ahrefs AI Visibility Checker asks for a brand name and reads none of your pages. The OperStack [free check](/ai-visibility/) asks for your address, reads up to ten pages sampled from your sitemap, and scores each one on the five things that decide whether an AI answer can quote it. Watch re-reads ten pages every week. Ahrefs Site Audit crawls far more pages, but it looks for SEO issues, and the Ahrefs pages we read do not describe it checking whether a page opens with an answer an AI system could lift.

**2. It writes the paragraph for you.** If a page it reads lacks a short opening answer, the free check drafts one from what your own pages say, with a "Copy the text" button under it. Watch sends the fix list for the whole site, each item with the page address and the words to paste. The writing help Ahrefs sells for existing pages is titles and meta descriptions, through Ask AI in Project Boost Pro at $20 a month per project. Ready answer paragraphs are not stated on its pages.

**3. It names who ChatGPT recommended instead, even for free.** The free check asks ChatGPT three buyer questions with web search on and lists the names it gave, sorted into real competitors, non-competitors such as portals and law firms, and names it could not identify. The free Ahrefs checker has no competitor comparison; Ahrefs keeps that for paid Brand Radar.

**4. It sends one email a week with what changed.** Every 7 days from the day you subscribe, Watch emails whether ChatGPT named you for each of ten questions, who it named in your place, how three rivals measure up beside you and what moved since the last letter. Ahrefs emails Rank Tracker reports weekly or monthly; a weekly Brand Radar email is not stated on the pages we read.

**5. Its free ChatGPT questions are asked live.** Each free check puts three questions to ChatGPT at the moment you run it, allows up to three such runs per connection per day, and shows the saved answer if you repeat the same site within 24 hours. The Ahrefs checker shows a top-five preview drawn from its index, and its page does not make clear whether each lookup runs live.

**6. It costs $35 a month, not hundreds.** Watch is $35 a month or $299 a year on our [pricing page](/pricing/). The cheapest Ahrefs route with tracked AI prompts is Lite at $129 a month for 5 prompts, and the index costs $199 a month for one AI platform or $699 for all of them. Lite plus the all-platform index comes to $828 a month (our arithmetic).

**7. It needs no developer and no SEO skills.** Nearly every fix arrives as text you paste yourself; where a change needs a developer, the letter flags it. It is written for the person who owns the business, not for a specialist reading a dashboard.

**8. You can try it and leave.** The first 7 days of Watch are free and you can cancel any time. Ahrefs has no free trial, and its pricing page says it does not issue refunds in general.

## What is Ahrefs for, and what is OperStack for?

Ahrefs is an SEO data platform with AI tracking added as a paid module; OperStack is an AI visibility tool that does one job.

### Ahrefs, in plain words

Ahrefs Pte. Ltd., based in Singapore, sells a toolkit for people who work on search every day: Site Explorer for links and traffic, Keywords Explorer for search demand, Site Audit for technical problems, Rank Tracker for positions, AI content tools, and Brand Radar for how AI answers mention a brand. Its main plans run from Lite, which Ahrefs labels for small businesses and personal projects, to Enterprise for large teams, with Starter at $29 below them for SEO beginners.

OperStack has two parts: the free check described above, and Watch, which turns it into a weekly letter with ten questions, three rivals and the fix list. It holds no keyword or backlink data. ## Is Ahrefs Brand Radar worth it for a small business?

For a small local business, usually not. Ahrefs itself warns that the index "may have limited coverage for brands with little or no search volume", and the custom prompts that would fill that gap are capped at 5 on Lite. A $35 weekly check on ChatGPT, with the fix written out, is the more direct route for most owners.

The reason lies in how the index is built. Brand Radar draws its prompts from Ahrefs keyword data: 449 million of them according to the Brand Radar page, while the pricing page says 475 million, and we give both because the two pages disagree. The prompts are re-tested monthly over a 90-day window. A family law office serving one county may turn up in none of them, because its category is never typed into Google often enough to become an index prompt. A dental clinic in one suburb may be in the same position.

Custom prompts are the workaround, and on Lite the arithmetic is tight. Ahrefs counts one prompt on one platform in one location as one check. Five prompts run daily on ChatGPT use the whole 150-check allowance, so adding a second platform means cutting prompts or buying a pack. One Claude update costs 8 checks. At $129 for 150 checks, each check works out at about 86 cents (our arithmetic). And none of those checks reads your pages or writes the text that would change the answer.

A quick test before paying: type your brand into the free Ahrefs checker, which runs on the same index. If it comes back with almost nothing, the paid index will not have much more on you. Then run the OperStack [free check](/ai-visibility/) on your address: three buyer questions asked the way your buyers ask them, answered live by ChatGPT, with the names it gave in your place.

## How does each tool check AI visibility?

Ahrefs looks at AI visibility from the brand's side: how often a name appears across a large set of prompts, and which domains and pages those answers cite. OperStack looks at it from the page's side: it asks ChatGPT ten buyer questions a week and reads ten of your pages to find what stops them from being quoted.

| | Ahrefs Brand Radar | OperStack Watch |
|---|---|---|
| What you enter | A brand name | A site address |
| Questions | Index prompts built from keyword data; custom prompts from 5 (Lite) | 10 buyer questions, unchanged week to week |
| How often | Index re-tested monthly; custom prompts daily, weekly or monthly | Every 7 days from your subscription date |
| Competitors | Share of voice against the competitors you track | Names ChatGPT gave instead of you, plus 3 rivals measured beside you |
| Your own pages | Pages that AI answers cite are listed | 10 pages scored on five areas every week |
| What you act on | Mentions, cited domains and cited pages | A page address and a paragraph to paste |

The OperStack score is the layer that turns a mention count into a to-do list. It is built from five areas with published weights, so you can add it up by hand. Access, 25 points, comes from the robots.txt rules for 14 AI crawlers and fetchers. A map for agents, 15 points, comes from llms.txt. Entity, 20 points, comes from business schema, FAQ markup, page type, canonical tags and Open Graph. Quotable content, 25 points, asks for 300 or more words, an answer-first paragraph with a figure right after the H1, at least three H2 sections and a table. Dates and sources, 15 points, looks for dates in meta or schema, named sources and sitemap dates. Brand Radar tells you whether you are mentioned. The score tells you what on the page stands in the way, and Watch writes the change.

A note on Ahrefs' own pages, because they disagree with each other. A help-center article, "What is Ahrefs", lists ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Grok and mentions seven AI indexes, while the Brand Radar page has no Grok. The free checker's introduction names five platforms and its FAQ adds AI Mode as a sixth. For the table above we used the Brand Radar page.

Who writes the prompts differs too. In Ahrefs you type them or let AI generate them, then decide which to keep. In Watch the ten questions are the kind your buyers ask, and they stay fixed, so a change between weeks reflects the answer, not a new question.

## What each tool tells a business owner on a Monday morning

An Ahrefs user starts the week with ranking emails, backlink alerts and a Brand Radar dashboard to interpret; an OperStack Watch subscriber gets one email naming who ChatGPT recommended instead of them and a paragraph to put on a named page.

**In Ahrefs**, the emails are about SEO: Rank Tracker reports, alerts for new and lost backlinks, and Report Builder PDFs, which are not on Free or Starter and cost $99 a month as an add-on. A weekly Brand Radar email is not stated on any page we read, so the AI part is a dashboard you open yourself. It shows where you stand. It does not hand you the sentence to write.

**In OperStack Watch**, the email is the product. The report shown above is a real first week for a Phuket property brand we run: named in 5 of the 10 questions, the two firms ChatGPT preferred each named in 3 answers, a score of 89 out of 100 across ten pages, and a fix of the week that starts by saying where on the site the text goes, followed by a paragraph built only from the site's own pages and a short note on what to confirm before publishing.

Here is what the owner does with that letter, in order:

1. Read the ten questions and see which ones went to someone else, and to whom. That list shows which rivals ChatGPT prefers for each question.
2. Open the fix of the week. It names the page and the spot on the page.
3. Check the paragraph against the business. Every sentence comes from the site's own pages, so the check is about whether it is still current, not whether it was invented.
4. Paste it. No developer, no ticket, no brief for a writer.
5. Next week, look at the same ten questions again and see whether anything moved.

Two caveats. Watch letters go every 7 days counted from the day you subscribed, so your Monday is whatever weekday you started. And ChatGPT answers vary between runs, which is why the questions stay the same and the report shows a trend rather than one verdict. If you want to see the first half of this before paying anything, the [free check](/ai-visibility/) asks three such questions about your site today.

## When you need a different kind of tool

Some jobs belong to an SEO suite, and OperStack does not try to do them. If a team plans content from keyword volumes (Ahrefs lists 28.7 billion filtered keywords in 217 locations on its Big Data page), runs link building from a backlink index (35 trillion external backlinks in its history), reports rankings for hundreds of keywords (750 on Lite), or crawls a site with thousands of pages for technical faults (100,000 crawl credits a month on Lite), that is search engine optimization. OperStack has no keyword database, no search volumes, no backlink index and no rank tracker, and it reads ten pages rather than crawling a whole catalog.

A business can keep an SEO suite, or an agency that runs one, for that work, and use OperStack for the separate question of whether ChatGPT recommends them and what to change on the page. The two sit on different lines of the budget. Our comparison with [hiring an SEO agency](/compare/operstack-vs-seo-agency/) covers the case where someone else does the work for you.

## Which one fits a clinic, an agency, a SaaS company or an online shop?

For the AI visibility question, OperStack Watch fits all four at $35 a month per site; what changes from one business to the next is what else they need from an SEO suite.

### A dental clinic or a small law office

The owner has no time for dashboards, and the brand has little search volume, which is exactly where Ahrefs says its index is thin. Watch asks ChatGPT the questions patients or clients ask, names the practices it recommends instead, and returns text to paste on the service pages. If an agency already handles the clinic's rankings, that stays a separate job; Watch adds the ChatGPT side without anyone learning a new tool.

### An agency with ten clients

Ahrefs Standard, at $249 a month, includes 10 tracked AI prompts across its 20 projects: one prompt per client if you have ten. Watch gives each client ten questions every week, the named rivals and a fix list the agency can deliver as finished work, and ten subscriptions come to $350 a month (our arithmetic; there is no agency tier on our [pricing page](/pricing/) as of 26 September 2026). For pitches, the [MCP server](/mcp/) is free with no account, and one of its five tools lines up between two and four sites on one 100-point scale, from inside Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor.

### A SaaS company

Software buyers ask ChatGPT for alternatives and shortlists, and Watch shows each week which products it names instead of yours, then points to the page and paragraph that could change the answer. Keyword research for a content calendar is a different task and sits with a suite.

### An online shop

Watch answers a narrow but valuable question for a shop: whether ChatGPT names the store when buyers ask where to get a product type, and who it names instead. Keyword data for thousands of category and product pages, and a crawl of the whole catalog, are SEO work for a suite.

## Using Ahrefs and OperStack together, or switching

If you already pay for Ahrefs, keep it for SEO data and move the AI visibility question to OperStack: the two tools overlap on very little, and Watch costs less than a single Brand Radar platform.

**What to keep in Ahrefs:** Site Explorer, Keywords Explorer, Rank Tracker and Site Audit. Nothing in OperStack replaces them.

**What to question:** a Brand Radar add-on bought mainly to watch ChatGPT. At $199 a month for one platform, it costs more than five months of Watch, and it neither reads your pages nor writes the fix.

**What OperStack adds:** ten pages read and scored every week, the rivals named, and the paragraph to paste. Ahrefs' Patches rewrite titles and meta descriptions; Watch writes the opening answers and other on-page text that AI systems quote. Watch asks ChatGPT only: it reads whether the crawlers of other AI engines can reach your site, but it does not track their answers week by week.

**How to move without a gap:**

1. Run the free check on your site. It costs nothing and shows three live ChatGPT answers.
2. Start Watch. Within the first 7 days you pay nothing.
3. If you drop the Brand Radar add-on or a plan, cancel from Account Settings. The plan runs to the end of the paid period, then the account becomes Ahrefs Free, which keeps Site Explorer and Site Audit for verified sites with 5,000 crawl credits a month per project. Ahrefs does not issue refunds in general, so time the cancellation to the end of a billing period.

Both MCP servers can sit side by side in Claude or Cursor. The Ahrefs one needs a paid plan and, for Brand Radar data, the add-on; ours needs no account and no plan. ## What our data says about small-business sites

Our study of 496 small-business sites found that 43% had no short, direct answer at the top of any page we checked, and 42% had no working sitemap at the usual addresses.
