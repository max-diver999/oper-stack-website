## What are OperStack and Peec AI for?

OperStack Watch supplies 10 weekly ChatGPT questions, page checks and proposed text for $35 a month. Peec Starter lists 50 daily prompts across 3 models for $95 monthly in the recorded pricing review. Watch is designed around recurring page work; Peec provides a broader set of observations and analytics.

1. **Your pages get read and scored.** The OperStack [free check](/ai-visibility/) takes your address, picks up to ten pages from your sitemap, and gives them a score out of 100 for whether an AI system can open, identify and quote them. Watch repeats that on ten pages every week. How many of your pages Peec reads is not stated: its docs describe a site audit group inside Actions and a robots.txt check for AI bots, without a page count.

2. **You get the paragraph, not the brief.** Peec's Actions list ranks what to work on and gives each item "The brief" for structure and "What to do" steps. Its own FAQ answers yes to the question of whether you still create the content yourself. OperStack writes it: when a page has no short answer near the top, the free check drafts one from the facts on your own pages, and Watch selects one priority fix from the sampled pages each week, with its address and proposed text. Every sentence comes from what your own site already says.

3. **There is no prompt list to build.** Peec asks you to write or choose your prompts, 50 of them on Starter, then pick three models. Watch uses ten questions of the kind a buyer of your type of business asks, and keeps them fixed from week to week so the trend means something.

4. **You can see a result before any account exists.** Peec has no free plan, and we found no public free checker on its site or in its sitemap; the trial starts with an account through Google, Microsoft, a work email link or SSO. The OperStack free check needs nothing: it runs three live ChatGPT questions with web search on and shows who was named, split into three groups: real rivals, names that are not rivals (portals, law firms and the like), and names it could not place.

5. **The weekly report arrives by email.** A built-in weekly email is not stated on Peec's pages. Its week-over-week digest, called Weekly Visibility Pulse, is an MCP prompt you run inside Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor, or schedule yourself as a task that posts to Slack. Watch simply emails you every 7 days.

6. **The price is about a third of Peec Starter's.** On our [pricing page](/pricing/), Watch is listed at $35 a month, or $299 for a full year. Peec Starter is $95 billed monthly or $960 a year billed annually (our arithmetic from $80 a month), and a fourth AI model adds $30 a month.

7. **Nothing to connect.** Peec's bot analytics need a CDN or log connection and its referral numbers need GA4. Watch needs a site address. Its fixes are almost all words to paste, and anything that truly needs a developer is labeled.

8. **Leaving is simple.** Watch charges nothing for the first 7 days and stops whenever you cancel. On a Peec 12-month plan, the terms ask for 30 days' notice before the year ends, and there is no automatic refund for ending a fixed term early.

OperStack starts from the site, not from a prompt list. The free check scores ten pages and asks ChatGPT three buyer questions. It asks ChatGPT only, and it carries no keyword or backlink data.

## Peec AI or OperStack for a clinic, an agency, a SaaS company or a shop?

A clinic, a 10-client agency, a SaaS business and a shop have different reporting requirements. Watch suits a small weekly set of ChatGPT questions tied to page edits. Peec covers more daily prompts and models. Select a scope your team can use, and retain any required engine or product coverage.

1. **A clinic owner**

A clinic has a short list of questions that matter: which practice to see for a given treatment, in a given area, at a given price. Watch asks ten of that kind every week, names the clinics ChatGPT recommends instead, and sends the paragraph for the treatment page. Peec Starter would ask the owner to write or pick 50 prompts, read a dashboard and still create the content. If you are also looking at other trackers, see how OperStack compares with [Otterly AI](/compare/operstack-vs-otterly-ai/) and [ContextBolt](/compare/operstack-vs-contextbolt/).

2. **An agency with ten clients**

Peec's agency plans are billed annually from $205 a month, and the content work for each client still falls on the agency. With Watch, each client gets a weekly letter with named rivals and a priority page fix the agency can hand over as finished work. There is no agency tier on our pricing, so ten client sites on Watch cost $350 a month by our count, with no annual commitment. In pitches, the free [MCP server](/mcp/) puts the prospect and up to three rivals on one 100-point scale inside Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor, with no account.

3. **A SaaS company**

People choosing software ask ChatGPT for shortlists and alternatives, and a product it names may enter the buyer's consideration set. Watch reports that name every week and points to the comparison or pricing page that needs a clearer opening answer. Keep the date and the buyer question with each observation.

4. **An online shop**

For a shop, Watch answers the store-level question: when buyers ask ChatGPT where to get a product type, is your store named, and who is named instead. Product-by-product tracking across a catalog is a different job and sits outside OperStack.

## Running Peec and OperStack side by side, or moving over

Adding Watch beside Peec supplies a weekly email and proposed page edits for $35 monthly. Replacing Peec also changes the engines, frequency and history available. Before cancellation, inspect those differences and the contract term; keeping useful records is part of the move even when the new report is simpler.

**What Watch adds next to Peec:** the paragraph for each weak page, written from your own site, and the ten-page score that shows which technical item is holding a page back. ## What our study recorded about supporting evidence

Our 496-site study retained only the 3 worst findings for each domain, so some reported issue shares are lower bounds. At least 21% lacked links to trust profiles and at least 13% stated figures without naming a source. These figures describe recorded page evidence, not the frequency of missing ChatGPT recommendations.

Source: OperStack free check study, 25 September 2026, on small-business websites in the United States, United Kingdom, Australia and Canada. Because the retained records were limited, an unlisted issue cannot be treated as an issue that passed inspection.

| Finding retained in the study | Minimum share of scored sites |
|---|---|
| Missing trust-profile links | 21% |
| Images without alternative text | 20% |
| Missing or broken llms.txt | 18% |
| Figures without an attributed source | 13% |

Those rows suggest different editing decisions. A trust link should identify a real business profile or professional credential. Alternative text should communicate the image's relevant content. A map should lead to current pages. A numerical claim should identify its source, scope and date. Adding generic words to all four places would not resolve the underlying problem.

For a team already using Peec Actions, compare the action with the affected page before drafting. Some template actions contain concrete text; others require new writing. Watch's proposed wording also needs review against what the business currently provides, even when it was derived from the website.

A practical review can start with one commercial claim: follow its link, check that the source supports the wording and preserve any limitation. Use our [cases page](/cases/) for context about owned examples, then maintain a separate record of your business's edits and question results. A finding count is not a forecast of leads.
