## How does each tool check AI visibility?

Profound runs many prompts on several engines every day and keeps the history; OperStack asks ChatGPT a fixed set of buyer questions each week and turns what it finds into edits.

| How often | Daily | Every 7 days |
| Questions | 50 a day on the Trial, tailored on Enterprise | 10 buyer questions |

| Your pages | Per-page readiness grade, page count not stated | 10 pages, score out of 100 |

The OperStack score has five parts with fixed weights, so a change is easy to trace. The first 25 points go to access: whether your robots.txt lets in each of 14 named AI crawlers and fetchers. The agent map, worth 15, checks for a working llms.txt. Entity, worth 20, looks for Organization or business schema, FAQPage, the page type, a canonical tag and Open Graph. Quotable content, worth 25, wants at least 300 words, a short answer with a figure right after the H1, three or more H2 sections and a table. Dates and sources, worth 15, look for dates in meta or schema, phrases that name a source, and lastmod in the sitemap. An SEO basics score on the same pages sits beside it.

The names matter as much as the number. When the free check asks ChatGPT its three questions, it files each name it finds under one of three labels: a genuine rival, a name that is no rival at all (a portal, say, or a law firm the answer happened to cite), or a name the check could not place. Watch keeps that count across ten questions, so a rival who turns up three times out of ten stands out from one who appeared once.

Why ten questions, and why weekly? ChatGPT rarely gives the same answer twice: put one buyer question to it in the morning and again after lunch and the firms it lists can differ. A single check is therefore a sample. Ten fixed questions, asked again every seven days, turn that noise into a trend you can act on. If a site goes from 5 of 10 to 7 of 10 after two pasted paragraphs, the letter shows the jump and which questions moved.

Whether assistants visit your pages is a different question from whether they name you. For that we run a free [AI visit counter](/visits/): a line on your pages counts visits from AI systems, and the MCP server can read your own count back with a private token. It is a counter, not an analytics suite, and it costs nothing.

Profound's Enterprise engines are ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, DeepSeek, Claude, Google AI Overviews and Exa Search. Watch asks ChatGPT only, and we would rather say that plainly than blur it.

## What does the fix OperStack writes actually look like?

It is a finished paragraph with an address: the question it answers, the page and position where it goes, and the text itself, followed by a note on anything to check before publishing.

In the report above, the instruction was to add the paragraph to the homepage just below the first section, and a closing note asked the owner to add one word if it described what the business sells. Every sentence in that paragraph came from the site's own pages, and each one is checked against a quote before it is allowed in. That rule is the point. A paragraph an assistant can lift is only safe to publish if every claim in it is already true on your site.

The free check does a smaller version at no cost. It writes out one fix in full, split into what to do now, the task and how to confirm it worked, and lists the other findings with a count. Where a page has no short answer at the top, it drafts one and gives you a copy button. Watch then covers the whole site every week, and marks the rare fix that needs a developer, so you know which items to hand over.

For businesses that prefer the work done, we quote fixes and setup separately, but only after the list of work is agreed in writing. Because most fixes are text, most owners can publish them without that.
