## What are OperStack and ContextBolt for?

OperStack Watch runs 10 ChatGPT questions weekly and sends written page fixes to an inbox for $35 monthly. ContextBolt SEO also costs $35, with research requested through an AI client and paid in credits. Its documentation describes reports started by the user, rather than an automatic weekly reporting service.

| What a business owner needs | OperStack | ContextBolt SEO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A check that runs without you | Every 7 days from the day you subscribe | Nothing runs on its own |
| A weekly email | Yes, with what changed since last week | Not offered |
| Fixed buyer questions, asked live | Ten, web search on, same wording each week | No fixed set; one live question per engine in a full report |
| Pages read free | Up to 10, sampled from your sitemap | 1 URL (GEO Audit) or the homepage (site check) |
| Live ChatGPT questions free | Three per run, no signup | One search you pick, one new site a day |
| Fix format | Text to paste, with the page address | A prompt to hand to your own AI agent |
| What you must already have | An email inbox | A compatible AI app and a connector |
| Twelve months | $299 | $420 |

### It runs every week without you

A Watch report is produced every seven days from the day you subscribed, whether or not anyone logs in. ContextBolt is built the other way round. Its documentation says "Every report is your call, made in your agent", and its own comparison page against Otterly answers the question "Does ContextBolt monitor prompts on a schedule?" with "No." The suggested rhythm is a report about once a month, and a routine exists only if you build one inside your own agent. For a busy owner that gap is the whole difference: a check nobody remembers to run is a check that never happens.

### It asks ChatGPT the same ten questions, live, each time

Watch asks ChatGPT ten questions a buyer of your kind of business would ask, with web search switched on, and keeps the wording fixed from week to week. ChatGPT's answers vary between runs, so repetition is what turns a pile of single answers into a trend you can read. ContextBolt keeps no fixed prompt set and offers no prompt slots. For ChatGPT and Google AI its report shows "the questions in the index where your brand appears", drawn from a stored index of past answers, so the index decides which questions you see rather than you.

### It names who ChatGPT picks instead, and scores three rivals

Every Watch report lists, by name, the businesses ChatGPT recommended in your place, and measures three rivals beside you on the same 100-point scale. The free check sorts the names from its three ChatGPT answers into competitors, not-competitors such as law firms and portals, and names it could not identify, so you can review directories separately from proposed rivals. In ContextBolt's paid report, share of voice and rank exist only against competitors you type in yourself, up to four. Name none and you get no share of voice or rank.

### It writes the text, using only your own pages

This is the difference owners notice first. If a page the free check reads lacks a short answer at its top, OperStack drafts an opening paragraph for it, ready to paste and built only from what the site's own pages say, with a "Copy the text" button beside it. Watch selects a priority fix from the sampled pages each week: it carries the page address and the words to review, and a fix that needs a developer is marked. The report shown above ends that way, with a finished paragraph and the instruction "Add this to your homepage, just below the first section".

ContextBolt does not write page text. Its audit gives "a specific fix" per issue, its AI visibility "What to do" list is a set of prompts labelled "Hand to your agent", and the free check on its SEO page offers "Copy the plan" and "Copy the prompt". Someone still has to paste that prompt into an AI app, judge what the app writes, and then change the page.

### It needs no AI app, connector or developer

Using ContextBolt means a compatible AI app with its own plan requirements, a custom connector, a sign-in by email code, and an active trial or subscription before the connection works at all. Its fixes are made by your agent "with your files", which assumes the agent can reach the files your site is built from. OperStack needs an email address. Fixes arrive as plain text, so whoever edits the site, the owner, an assistant or a freelancer, can apply them in whatever system the site runs on.

### What is ContextBolt SEO, and what is OperStack?

ContextBolt SEO is a hosted MCP server for SEO research inside an AI app; OperStack is AI visibility software with a free check and a paid weekly report called Watch.

### OperStack

OperStack is made for small and mid-size businesses and the agencies that serve them. The [free check](/ai-visibility/) needs no signup and nothing installed: it samples up to ten pages from your sitemap, scores them out of 100 across five areas, writes one fix out in full, and asks ChatGPT three buyer questions. OperStack Watch repeats that work every week at a larger scale for [$35 a month or $299 a year](/pricing/). Maksim Shchegolev, who owns OperStack, answers support himself at info@oper-stack.com. What OperStack does not have: a keyword database, search volumes, a backlink index or rank tracking.

## How does each tool check whether ChatGPT names you?

OperStack uses 10 live ChatGPT questions per Watch cycle and separately examines a page sample. ContextBolt combines an index of ChatGPT and Google AI answers with live questions to 3 other engines in its full report. Those are different observations, so their counts should retain their original labels.

In ContextBolt, ChatGPT and Google AI are measured from its answer index, which carries 12 months of history for those two. Perplexity, Claude and Gemini are each asked once, with a question built from "best" plus your topic, at the moment the report runs. The free AI Visibility Checker reads the same kind of index, "a large index of real ChatGPT and Google AI answers" in its own words, rather than asking anything live, and allows two checks without signup. The only live ChatGPT search on ContextBolt's free side is the site check on its SEO page: one search you pick, one new site a day, reading your homepage.

In OperStack, every question is asked live. The free check asks three and allows three such runs per connection per day; asking about the same site again within 24 hours shows the saved answer instead of spending a new run. Watch asks ten. Each answer is read for whether your business was named and for every other name ChatGPT gave.

Next to the answers, Watch scores ten of your pages on the same five areas as the free check:

1. **Access, 25 points:** the robots.txt rules for 14 AI crawlers and fetchers, read one user agent at a time.
1. **Agent map, 15:** whether llms.txt exists and works.
1. **Entity, 20:** business schema, FAQPage, page type, canonical and Open Graph.
1. **Quotable content, 25:** pages of 300 words or more that open with a direct answer carrying a number, split under at least three H2 headings, with a table somewhere on the page.
1. **Dates and sources, 15:** a publication or update date a machine can read, wording that says where each figure comes from, and lastmod values in the sitemap.

The score identifies page findings worth reviewing. The named rivals show who gains when it does. One limit, stated plainly: Watch asks ChatGPT only. For Claude, Perplexity and Gemini it checks whether your robots.txt lets their crawlers in, not what they answer.

## Which tool suits a dental clinic, a ten-client agency, a software company or a web shop?

Across 4 business types, the practical distinction is who will run the research and edit the site. Watch suits an owner who wants a recurring inbox report. ContextBolt suits an existing agent workflow. Neither subscription supplies an employee, and both require someone to approve changes before they reach customers.

1. **A clinic owner.** A dental or aesthetics clinic is where an MCP-only product breaks down, because the owner is not going to add a custom connector to Cursor between appointments. Run the free check first. It reads ten pages, asks ChatGPT three patient-style questions and shows which clinics were named instead, with booking portals and directories set apart from real rivals. If ChatGPT names three nearby clinics and not yours, Watch sends a fix each week as a paragraph the receptionist or the web person can paste the same day.

2. **An agency with ten clients.** The agency problem is staff hours. A tool where "every report is your call" means a person runs ten reports by hand and turns them into something a client can read, and ContextBolt's page for solo founders says you do not get "scheduled rank tracking or white-label client reports". Watch sends each client site its own letter with named rivals and pasteable text, which is close to a client deliverable as it arrives. Our pricing page lists one Watch plan and no agency tier. For winning new accounts, the $149 audit (a hundred pages, five rivals, reviewed by a person, no subscription) and the free [MCP server](/mcp/), which compares two to four sites on the same 100-point scale inside Claude or Cursor, are the pieces agencies use in a pitch.

3. **A SaaS company.** Software buyers ask ChatGPT which tool to use for a job, and in a crowded category the answer spreads across many names, so keep the question and date with each observation. A SaaS team can use the list of rivals ChatGPT names for the same buyer questions, and a dated record of whether that list moves after a page is edited. That is Watch. If the marketing team also does keyword research in Cursor, a research tool can sit beside it; it answers a different question.

4. **An online shop.** A shop that sells through advice, where a buyer asks "which one should I get?", depends on whether ChatGPT names it. Watch asks those questions weekly and scores your key pages. Ten pages are a sample, not a catalogue of thousands of products, so for the category and product templates the $149 audit reads a hundred. Keyword volume research for product pages is the separate job described above.
