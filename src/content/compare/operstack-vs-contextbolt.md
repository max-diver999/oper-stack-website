ContextBolt and OperStack Watch cost the same $35 a month, both give seven days free, and both ask for a card at checkout. There the likeness ends. OperStack is our product, built to answer one question for a business owner every week without being asked: does ChatGPT name you, who does it name instead, and what should your page say so that changes. ContextBolt SEO is an SEO research kit that lives inside Claude, Cursor or Codex and does nothing until someone types a request. Every ContextBolt detail below comes from ContextBolt's own pages, or from one interview with its founder, as we read them on 26 September 2026; the addresses are in the table above and the source list below.

## What does OperStack do for AI visibility that ContextBolt does not?

OperStack runs on a schedule, asks ChatGPT the same ten live buyer questions every week, names who it recommends instead of you, and sends the fix as finished text by email; ContextBolt does none of these by itself.

### It runs every week without you

A Watch report is produced every seven days from the day you subscribed, whether or not anyone logs in. ContextBolt is built the other way round. Its documentation says "Every report is your call, made in your agent", and its own comparison page against Otterly answers the question "Does ContextBolt monitor prompts on a schedule?" with "No." The suggested rhythm is a report about once a month, and a routine exists only if you build one inside your own agent. For a busy owner that gap is the whole difference: a check nobody remembers to run is a check that never happens.

### It asks ChatGPT the same ten questions, live, each time

Watch asks ChatGPT ten questions a buyer of your kind of business would ask, with web search switched on, and keeps the wording fixed from week to week. ChatGPT's answers vary between runs, so repetition is what turns a pile of single answers into a trend you can read. ContextBolt keeps no fixed prompt set and offers no prompt slots. For ChatGPT and Google AI its report shows "the questions in the index where your brand appears", drawn from a stored index of past answers, so the index decides which questions you see rather than you.

### It names who ChatGPT picks instead, and scores three rivals

Every Watch report lists, by name, the businesses ChatGPT recommended in your place, and measures three rivals beside you on the same 100-point scale. The free check sorts the names from its three ChatGPT answers into competitors, not-competitors such as law firms and portals, and names it could not identify, so a directory listing is never mistaken for a rival. In ContextBolt's paid report, share of voice and rank exist only against competitors you type in yourself, up to four. Name none and you get no share of voice or rank.

### It writes the text, using only your own pages

This is the difference owners notice first. If a page the free check reads lacks a short answer at its top, OperStack drafts an opening paragraph for it, ready to paste and built only from what the site's own pages say, with a "Copy the text" button beside it. Watch does the same across the whole site: each fix carries the page address and the words to paste, and the rare fix that needs a developer is marked. The report shown above ends that way, with a finished paragraph and the instruction "Add this to your homepage, just below the first section".

ContextBolt does not write page text. Its audit gives "a specific fix" per issue, its AI visibility "What to do" list is a set of prompts labelled "Hand to your agent", and the free check on its SEO page offers "Copy the plan" and "Copy the prompt". Someone still has to paste that prompt into an AI app, judge what the app writes, and then change the page.

### It needs no AI app, connector or developer

Using ContextBolt means an MCP-capable AI app billed separately by its maker, a custom connector, a sign-in by email code, and an active trial or subscription before the connection works at all. Its fixes are made by your agent "with your files", which assumes the agent can reach the files your site is built from. OperStack needs an email address. Fixes arrive as plain text, so whoever edits the site, the owner, an assistant or a freelancer, can apply them in whatever system the site runs on.

## What is ContextBolt SEO, and what is OperStack?

ContextBolt SEO is a hosted MCP server for SEO research inside an AI app; OperStack is AI visibility software with a free check and a paid weekly report called Watch.

You connect ContextBolt to Claude, Cursor, Codex or one of the other clients it names, 13 in all, and ask SEO questions in plain sentences. It returns keyword, search result, domain, backlink, site audit and AI visibility data drawn from DataForSEO, which its docs describe as "decision-useful rather than an exact match", and saves each lookup to a private SEO Dashboard. The founder has said it launched in May 2026, and its terms describe it as "built and run by one person". There is a single plan with 1,000 credits a month, and every action spends credits.

OperStack is made for small and mid-size businesses and the agencies that serve them. The [free check](/ai-visibility/) needs no signup and nothing installed: it samples up to ten pages from your sitemap, scores them out of 100 across five areas, writes one fix out in full, and asks ChatGPT three buyer questions. OperStack Watch repeats that work every week at a larger scale for [$35 a month or $299 a year](/pricing/). Maksim Shchegolev, who owns OperStack, answers support himself at info@oper-stack.com. What OperStack does not have: a keyword database, search volumes, a backlink index or rank tracking.

## How does each tool check whether ChatGPT names you?

OperStack asks ChatGPT live, with web search on, and records every name in the answer; ContextBolt counts your brand in a stored index of ChatGPT and Google AI answers and adds one live question to three other engines when you run a full report.

In ContextBolt, ChatGPT and Google AI are measured from its answer index, which carries 12 months of history for those two. Perplexity, Claude and Gemini are each asked once, with a question built from "best" plus your topic, at the moment the report runs. The free AI Visibility Checker reads the same kind of index, "a large index of real ChatGPT and Google AI answers" in its own words, rather than asking anything live, and allows two checks without signup. The only live ChatGPT search on ContextBolt's free side is the site check on its SEO page: one search you pick, one new site a day, reading your homepage.

In OperStack, every question is asked live. The free check asks three and allows three such runs per connection per day; asking about the same site again within 24 hours shows the saved answer instead of spending a new run. Watch asks ten. Each answer is read for whether your business was named and for every other name ChatGPT gave.

Next to the answers, Watch scores ten of your pages on the same five areas as the free check:

- **Access, 25 points:** the robots.txt rules for 14 AI crawlers and fetchers, read one user agent at a time.
- **Agent map, 15:** whether llms.txt exists and works.
- **Entity, 20:** business schema, FAQPage, page type, canonical and Open Graph.
- **Quotable content, 25:** pages of 300 words or more that open with a direct answer carrying a number, split under at least three H2 headings, with a table somewhere on the page.
- **Dates and sources, 15:** a publication or update date a machine can read, wording that says where each figure comes from, and lastmod values in the sitemap.

The score explains why ChatGPT may be passing you over. The named rivals show who gains when it does. One limit, stated plainly: Watch asks ChatGPT only. For Claude, Perplexity and Gemini it checks whether your robots.txt lets their crawlers in, not what they answer.

## What lands on a business owner's desk on Monday morning?

With OperStack, Monday's answer is a letter already in the inbox if Monday is your cycle day, since letters go out every seven days from the day you subscribed rather than on a fixed weekday; with ContextBolt, nothing arrives until you open your AI app and ask.

The OperStack letter opens with what changed since last week. The report shown above, run for a Phuket property brand we run, shows the shape: 5 of 10 questions named the site, its ten pages scored 89 out of 100, and two firms were each named instead in 3 of the 10 answers. Then comes the fix of the week, a paragraph assembled from the site's own pages, with the question it answers and exactly where it goes. The owner reads it, then pastes the paragraph or forwards the letter to whoever edits the site. No dashboard to log into, no prompt to write, no connector to keep alive.

A ContextBolt Monday starts in Claude, Cursor or Codex. You type something like "how visible are we in AI answers against these two rivals?", the agent calls the full report, and 66 credits leave the balance, 50 for the report and 8 for each rival. According to the docs you get back the indexed questions where your brand appears, share of voice and rank against the two rivals, a "Who AI names" panel, one live answer each from Perplexity, Claude and Gemini, and a "What to do" list of prompts. The lookup is saved to the dashboard. ContextBolt's automation page presents the missing letter as a choice: "Ask what moved since last month instead of reading a weekly email." Whoever runs the agent then has to turn those prompts into changes on the page.

For a founder who spends the day in Cursor, that flow feels natural. For a clinic owner, a law firm partner or a shop manager it is a second job. The honest test is short: will anyone on your team open an AI agent every week and ask? If the answer is no, only one of these two tools will ever tell you anything.

ContextBolt is mainly an SEO research kit, and SEO research is a different job from knowing whether ChatGPT recommends you. If your team plans articles from keyword volumes and difficulty, checks the live Google top 10 before writing, or hunts for sites that link to a rival and not to you, that is keyword and backlink work, and ContextBolt sells it by the credit inside an AI agent. OperStack has no keyword database, no backlink index and no rank tracking, and we do not pretend it does. If what you are really buying is an SEO specialist's toolbox, compare the full suites as well: our pages on [Ahrefs](/compare/operstack-vs-ahrefs/) and [Semrush](/compare/operstack-vs-semrush/) cover that choice. If what you need is a weekly verdict on your standing in ChatGPT, named or not, which rivals got the mention and the wording to change, that is the job OperStack was built for, and it does that job without anyone on your team running a report.

## Which tool suits a dental clinic, a ten-client agency, a software company or a web shop?

For AI visibility, all four are better served by a check that runs itself and writes the fix; the only reason to add ContextBolt is keyword and link research, which is a separate job.

Run the free check first. It reads ten pages, asks ChatGPT three patient-style questions and shows which clinics were named instead, with booking portals and directories set apart from real rivals. If ChatGPT names three nearby clinics and not yours, Watch sends a fix each week as a paragraph the receptionist or the web person can paste the same day.

**An agency with ten clients.** The agency problem is staff hours. A tool where "every report is your call" means a person runs ten reports by hand and turns them into something a client can read, and ContextBolt's page for solo founders says you do not get "scheduled rank tracking or white-label client reports". Watch sends each client site its own letter with named rivals and pasteable text, which is close to a client deliverable as it arrives. Our pricing page lists one Watch plan and no agency tier. For winning new accounts, the $149 audit (a hundred pages, five rivals, reviewed by a person, no subscription) and the free [MCP server](/mcp/), which compares two to four sites on the same 100-point scale inside Claude or Cursor, are the pieces agencies use in a pitch.

**A SaaS company.** Software buyers ask ChatGPT which tool to use for a job, and in a crowded category the answer spreads across many names, as our own data below shows. What a SaaS team needs each week is the list of rivals ChatGPT names for the same buyer questions, and proof of whether that list moves after a page is edited. That is Watch.
