## What are OperStack and HubSpot AI Search Grader for?

OperStack combines up to 10 page checks with live ChatGPT questions and draft page text; Watch repeats its monitoring weekly. HubSpot AI Search Grader offers a free brand snapshot, followed by a separate paid AEO product. Distinguishing the grader from HubSpot AEO matters when comparing features, prices and ongoing monitoring.

1. **It reads ten of your pages, not a description of your brand.** The grader takes a company name, a location, an industry and a product, and HubSpot describes no crawl of your site. The [OperStack free check](/ai-visibility/) samples up to 10 pages from your sitemap and scores each one on crawler access, llms.txt, schema, quotable content and dates. The one-off $149 audit goes to 100 pages.

2. **It shows who beat you, by name.** The free check puts three buyer-style questions to ChatGPT and lists the businesses named in each answer, split into real competitors, bystanders such as portals or law firms, and names it could not place. The grader has a Share of Voice part worth 10 of its 100 points; whether the free report names those competitors is not stated on the page we read.

3. **It writes the words.** When a checked page opens without a short, plain answer, the free check drafts one for that page using only claims found on your own pages, with a "Copy the text" button. The grader gives a written interpretation and, according to HubSpot's AEO page, "a sample of recommendations".

4. **It asks today's ChatGPT, with search on.** HubSpot's AEO page says the grader's ChatGPT and Gemini answers come from training data. OperStack asks ChatGPT with web search switched on, the mode that finds what is on the web now.

5. **It needs no form.** You type a site address and the result appears on screen with a permanent link. The grader's full report needs six fields, phone number included.

6. **It keeps going every week.** OperStack Watch asks the same ten questions every seven days, measures three rivals beside you, re-scores ten pages and sends one email saying what changed. The grader is a snapshot you have to remember to repeat.

7. **It costs less and lets you leave.** Watch is $35 a month or $299 a year on the [pricing page](/pricing/), against $50 a month for standalone HubSpot AEO. OperStack's MCP server is free; HubSpot's AI data over MCP needs Marketing Hub Professional, from $800 a month plus $3,000 onboarding. Watch can be cancelled at any time. HubSpot's paid plans end only at the close of the term. And nearly every OperStack fix is a block of text: the handful that need a developer carry a label saying so.

### What are the two tools for?

HubSpot's grader is a brand reputation report built to lead into HubSpot's paid AEO product. OperStack is a page-level check plus weekly ChatGPT tracking for small and mid-size businesses and agencies.

AEO also comes bundled in HubSpot's larger plans, and this is where the gap in price becomes a gap in kind. For a small business that wants to know whether ChatGPT names it, those plans are a CRM purchase with AI visibility inside, not an AI visibility purchase.

Commitment differs as much as price. HubSpot's help center says paid subscriptions end only at the close of the commitment term, by switching off auto-renewal in Account & Billing, and mid-term cancellation is not allowed. Its terms say amounts paid are non-refundable except where the agreement provides otherwise, and section 2.12 says data in a free trial may be permanently deleted unless you buy before the trial ends. With Watch, cancelling during the first 7 days costs nothing, cancelling later simply stops the next charge, and periods already paid stay paid, as our [refund page](/refunds/) states.

### What does the HubSpot grader ask for before it shows the full report?

The first screen needs only company details. The full report unlocks after a second form, marked "All fields are required", that asks for first name, last name, email, phone number, website URL and number of employees.

We read those fields from the public form definition the grader page loads; its submit button reads "Download Now". We did not submit it, so this page shows no HubSpot result for any site, ours included. The form's privacy text says HubSpot uses the details to contact you about its content, products and services. An email ending in .edu adds a "What is your role?" question.

HubSpot's own comparison with Otterly describes the grader as "No sign-up, credit card, or technical setup required". That holds in a narrow sense: there is no account to create and no card to enter. A phone number is still part of the price of the full report, and the privacy text tells you to expect contact.

The OperStack free check asks for the site address and nothing else. The result appears on screen with a permanent link you can forward to a colleague or a client.

## How does OperStack check AI visibility, page by page?

OperStack scores website material separately from its live ChatGPT answers: 5 weighted areas describe the sampled pages, while the answers show which businesses were named. HubSpot Grader describes brand reputation and HubSpot AEO tracks prompts over time. None of these scores should be treated as interchangeable evidence of sales.

| Area of the score | Points | What it looks at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access | 25 | robots.txt rules, agent by agent, for 14 AI crawlers and fetchers |
| Agent map | 15 | Whether llms.txt exists and works |
| Entity | 20 | Organization or business schema, FAQPage, page type, canonical, Open Graph |
| Quotable content | 25 | Length of 300 words or more, a plain answer with a number directly under the H1, three or more H2 headings, a table |
| Dates and sources | 15 | Dates in meta or schema, phrases naming a source, sitemap lastmod |

On top of that, the same pages get an SEO basics score and a readiness line for each AI engine; engines differ there only by what your robots.txt lets in. One fix arrives written out in full, in three parts: the immediate step, the task behind it, and a way to confirm it took. You get three ChatGPT runs a day per connection, and checking the same site again inside 24 hours returns the stored answer rather than a new one.

| Step | HubSpot grader | OperStack free check | OperStack Watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Engines answering | OpenAI model, Perplexity, Gemini | ChatGPT, search on | ChatGPT, search on |
| How often | Once | On demand, 3 ChatGPT runs a day | Every 7 days |
| Questions | Pre-set, count not stated | 3 | 10 |
| Your pages | No crawl described | Up to 10 read and scored | 10 scored every week |
| Rivals | Share of Voice points | Named, sorted by type | Named, plus 3 measured beside you |
| Output you act on | Interpretation, sample recommendations | One full fix, opening paragraph | Priority page fix with page address and text |

What OperStack leaves out, stated plainly: it does not score sentiment, it has no keyword database, search volumes, backlink index or rank tracking, and it does not collect Claude, Perplexity or Gemini answers each week.

### What matters about the grader's collection method?

HubSpot's own pages list different OpenAI model names, so we do not infer the running model from those descriptions. Its AEO page says the grader combines training-data answers from ChatGPT and Gemini with live Perplexity search. Watch uses ChatGPT with web search enabled. These methods can yield different observations; a model label alone does not establish which result represents every customer. The source table preserves the official pages and review date.

### Is the HubSpot AI Search Grader accurate?

It is a fair reading of what it sets out to measure: how AI models talk about a brand in general. It cannot tell you what is wrong with your website, because HubSpot describes no crawl of your pages, so nothing in the report is tied to a page address.

Three details decide how to read the number.

**The weights lean on sentiment.** Sentiment is worth up to 40 points, Presence Quality 20, Brand Recognition 20, Share of Voice 10 and Market Competition 10. The total therefore says more about how warmly a brand is described than about whether it lands on a buyer's shortlist, which is mostly the job of the 10-point Share of Voice part. HubSpot scores that part as your estimated percentage of category voice plus a rank bonus.

**Rival names are not promised.** The grader page says Share of Voice shows "where your brand ranks relative to competitors". Whether the free report lists those competitors by name is not stated.

**It is one sample.** SparkToro and Gumshoe's January 2026 research showed that AI tools seldom repeat the same set of brands for an identical prompt, so any single reading carries noise. The same caution applies to our free check, which rests on three ChatGPT answers. That is exactly why Watch exists: the same ten questions, every week, so a trend replaces a guess.

## Clinic, agency, SaaS or online shop: which should you use?

The practical choice across 4 business types depends partly on whether HubSpot is already paid for. A clinic needs useful treatment-page findings; an agency needs separate client coverage; a software team may already have bundled AEO; a store must decide which parts of its catalogue a small sample can represent.

1. **A dental or aesthetics clinic**

A clinic's patients ask local questions, and the gaps we see on clinic sites are page-level. In our 496-site study, 44% of the dental practice sites had not one checked page that opened with a direct answer. Run the [free check](/ai-visibility/), paste the opening paragraph it drafts, and start Watch when you want to know, week by week, whether that paragraph moved you onto ChatGPT's list.

2. **An agency with ten clients**

Watch for ten clients costs $350 a month or $2,990 a year at list price, by our arithmetic, and each client gets its own weekly email and priority page fix you can forward. The free [MCP server](/mcp/) compares two to four client sites on one scale from inside Claude or Cursor at no cost, which suits a pitch. For a new client, the $149 audit reads 100 pages and five rivals and is reviewed by a person. On the HubSpot side, each full grader report sits behind a form with your contact details, and HubSpot states that each additional brand needs another $50 monthly AEO instance.

3. **A SaaS company**

If the company already pays for Marketing Hub Professional, AEO comes bundled with it. OperStack adds what HubSpot's AEO pages do not describe: a reading of your own pages and the replacement text with the page address attached. Engineers can wire OperStack's free MCP server into Cursor and score a page before it ships. If you are not on HubSpot, $35 a month for Watch against $50 for standalone AEO is the like-for-like choice.

4. **An online shop**

OperStack reads a sample of 10 pages per check, and the $149 audit reads 100, so start with the audit if your catalogue is large. Neither OperStack nor, going by HubSpot's pages, the grader or AEO provides keyword volumes or rank tracking, so product search demand still needs a search data tool.

## How do you move from the HubSpot grader to OperStack?

A HubSpot grader report and the first Watch email describe different observations, so keep both with their dates. Use the 7-day Watch trial to inspect its questions and proposed edits before the first charge. If ending paid HubSpot AEO, check the commitment term and save needed data before access ends.

1. If you already have a grader report, export it and file it. It is your reputation baseline from before any page changes.
2. Run the OperStack free check on the same site. Paste the opening paragraph it drafts on the page it names, and apply the one full fix.
3. Start Watch; the first 7 days are free. The first email lists the ten questions, who ChatGPT named for each, and the priority page fix.
4. Review the proposed fix and update one page a week. Each email says what changed since the last one.
5. If you were on HubSpot AEO, switch off auto-renewal in Account & Billing well before the term ends, and save anything from a trial before day 28. Your prompts tagged Decision make a good yardstick for whether Watch's ten questions match how your buyers ask.

If your team uses HubSpot's CRM, nothing here conflicts with it: OperStack's MCP server needs no account and can sit in the same Claude setup as any other connector. ## What the HubSpot homepage sample shows

The OperStack check scored HubSpot's homepage at 72 out of 100 on 26 September 2026, reading only 1 page. Its largest recorded shortfall was dates and sources. That is a website-readiness observation under our own rules, not a HubSpot Grader result or a judgement of HubSpot's paid software.

The figures below come from that stored run. The scope is deliberately explicit because a homepage observation cannot describe every HubSpot product page, help article or translated site. No HubSpot account or competitor-tool test was used to produce it.

| Area measured on the homepage | Points awarded | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Crawler access | 25 | 25 |
| Agent map | 15 | 15 |
| Entity and markup | 15 | 20 |
| Quotable content | 14 | 25 |
| Dates and sources | 3 | 15 |
| Total | 72 | 100 |

An owner can perform the useful part of the review without treating the total as a verdict. Open the page, locate the claim a customer would quote and look for an identifiable source and an honest date. Check whether an update date refers to a real content change rather than a build that ran this morning.

A brand-reputation score answers a different question. The words an assistant associates with a company do not reveal whether a particular service page has a date, a source or an accessible explanation. Conversely, adding those elements does not establish that sentiment or recommendations improved.
