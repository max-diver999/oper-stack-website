## What are OperStack and Google Search Console for?

OperStack adds 10 weekly ChatGPT questions and written page fixes to information Google Search Console cannot supply. Search Console remains the free record of your own Google search presence. The two products measure different systems, and their numbers answer different questions even when both reports refer to the same page.

| The owner's question | Google Search Console | OperStack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does ChatGPT recommend my business? | Not covered: the docs we read never mention ChatGPT | Ten buyer questions a week, web search on |
| Who gets recommended instead? | No data about other sites | Named in every answer; three rivals scored |
| What should the page say? | Issue names, URLs, links to Google's docs | A paragraph to paste and the page address |
| How many pages are checked at once? | URL Inspection: one address at a time | Up to ten, sampled from your sitemap |
| What arrives by email? | Alerts when Google finds an issue | A weekly letter with what changed |
| What do I need first? | A Google account and proof of ownership | Nothing, for the free check |

### Whether ChatGPT recommends you at all

Search Console's view of AI ends at Google's border: AI Overviews and AI Mode in Search, plus the generative features in Discover, which have their own report. None of the help pages we read mention ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude or the Gemini app. OperStack Watch goes straight to ChatGPT with ten questions a buyer of your kind of business would type, web search switched on, and the wording held steady from one week to the next. With question wording held fixed, changes are easier to investigate, although the answer can vary without any site being edited. The [free check](/ai-visibility/) asks three of those questions before you pay or register anything.

### The names ChatGPT gives in your place

Search Console reports on properties you can access. Watch records the names in its ChatGPT answers and measures three rivals on the same readiness scale. Review the proposed competitor classifications: a portal or professional adviser mentioned in an answer may be relevant without being a direct rival.

### A fix you can paste

When Google spots a problem, Search Console names the issue, lists the affected URLs, links to its documentation and lets you mark the repair with "Validate fix". Its Recommendations feature, announced as an experiment in August 2024, suggests actions such as adding structured data or a sitemap. It never hands you words for the page. OperStack does. Where a page lacks a short, direct answer near the top, the free check writes an opening paragraph from facts already on your own site and puts a "Copy the text" button under it. Watch extends that across the sampled pages: every item names the page and gives the text, and the handful that need a developer carry a flag.

### Ten pages in one pass, with no account

URL Inspection looks at one address at a time, with a daily cap per property; through the API the cap is 2,000 inspections a day per site. The OperStack free check reads up to ten pages sampled from your sitemap in a single run, scores how easily AI systems can read and quote them, and leaves a permanent link to the result. There is no Google account to connect and no ownership to prove. Search Console, by contrast, asks for an HTML file, a meta tag, an Analytics or Tag Manager code, a Google Sites or Blogger account, or a DNS record. Google calls DNS "more complex", yet it is the only method that verifies a Domain property.

### A letter that comes to you

Search Console writes when something breaks: email alerts for issues Google finds, set per user on the Email preferences page. The help pages we checked describe no regular performance summary by email; the weekly and monthly views sit inside the Performance report, waiting for you to open them. Watch sends one letter every seven days, counted from the day you subscribed, and it leads with what moved since the previous week.

### What is Search Console for, and what is OperStack for?

Search Console is Google's free record of how your site appears in Google Search; OperStack is AI visibility software that checks how ChatGPT treats your business and tells you what to fix.

Google Search Console gives an owner evidence of how a verified property appears in Google. ### OperStack

OperStack has two parts. The free check samples up to ten pages, scores them out of 100 across crawler access, llms.txt, entity markup, quotable content, and dates and sources, spells out one fix in full and puts three buyer questions to ChatGPT. OperStack Watch runs a larger version every week; the [pricing page](/pricing/) lists it at $35 a month or $299 a year with seven days free. OperStack holds no search data of any kind: no clicks, impressions, positions, keyword volumes, backlinks or indexing controls. For all of that, Search Console is the source.

## How do Search Console and OperStack measure AI visibility?

Google Search Console records impressions on Google surfaces, including AI Overviews and AI Mode. OperStack Watch samples 10 answers from ChatGPT every week and checks up to 10 website pages. Google impressions and sampled mentions use different denominators, so comparing their percentages directly would give a misleading result.

| | Google Search Console | OperStack Watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI systems | AI Overviews and AI Mode; Discover separately | ChatGPT, web search on |
| Questions | None you choose; the AI report has no query dimension | Ten fixed buyer questions a week; three in the free check |
| Pages | Whatever Google showed; no crawl on request | Ten from your sitemap, scored every week |
| Rivals | None | Every name in each answer; three rivals scored |
| Page readiness for AI | Not part of the AI report | robots.txt for 14 AI crawlers and fetchers, llms.txt, schema, answer-first text, dates |
| Fix text | None | A paragraph and a page address per fix |
| History | 16 months | Each letter compares with the week before |

Neither measure stands in for the other. Impressions in AI Overviews measure exposure on Google. Named mentions in ChatGPT measure recommendation inside an assistant. A business can do well on one and badly on the other, and that is the practical reason a small business owner should watch both.

### Does Google Search Console show AI Overviews traffic?

It shows impressions, not traffic: the Generative AI performance report, launched on 3 June 2026 and available to all websites worldwide as of 31 August 2026, counts how often links to your site were shown in AI Overviews and AI Mode on Google Search.

| The Generative AI report gives you | It does not give you |
| --- | --- |
| Impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode | Clicks or click-through rate |
| A split by page, country and device | The searches that triggered the appearance |
| Dates by hour, day, week or month | The other sites cited in the same answer |
| A search type filter and an export | Search Labs experiments, which are excluded |
| Discover's AI features, in a separate report | Anything from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude or the Gemini app |

Two details decide how you read it. The first is clicks. In the ordinary Performance report, AI Overviews and AI Mode are counted inside the Web search type: an AI Overview takes one position, every link inside it shares that position, and a click on one of those links is a normal click. So clicks from AI answers are already in your totals; the Generative AI report just does not break them out. The second is availability. The report needs a verified property and may not appear for a site with too few AI impressions, so a missing report can mean low exposure rather than a setup fault.

For anyone pulling data by code there is one more gap. The Search Analytics API lists the search types web, image, video, news, googleNews and discover, with no generative AI type, so API access to the new report is not stated. Google's page "AI features and your website", last updated on 10 December 2025, still describes only the older arrangement in which AI features sit inside Web.

Open the report every week, and read it as what it is: Google's count of how often it showed you inside its own AI answers, on its own search engine.

### Does Google Search Console track ChatGPT?

No. Search Console covers Google's surfaces only, and the documentation we checked never mentions ChatGPT, so it cannot tell you whether ChatGPT recommends you, whom it recommends instead or what to change.

A buyer who asks ChatGPT for a shortlist of accountants, clinics or software tools gets names in a chat window, and nothing about that conversation reaches your Search Console account. If ChatGPT then sends the buyer to your site, the visit did not come from Google Search either, so Search Console does not report it. OperStack approaches this from both ends. Watch puts the questions to ChatGPT directly and records who was named, and our separate free [visit counter](/visits/) counts visits that arrive from AI systems.

The gap matters most where buyers pick from a short list: a patient choosing between three clinics, a founder choosing between three tools, a family choosing between three law firms. Appearing in Google's AI Overview for a question helps. Being the name ChatGPT gives when someone asks for a recommendation is a separate measure, and only a tool that asks ChatGPT can see it. If you have been doing that by hand, our page on [asking ChatGPT yourself](/compare/operstack-vs-asking-chatgpt/) covers what a manual check misses.

## Search Console and OperStack for clinics, agencies, SaaS teams and shops

For 4 common business types, start by identifying the pages and market that produce enquiries. Search Console supplies Google records for verified properties. Watch adds a limited ChatGPT question sample. A clinic, an agency, a software company and a store should select coverage around their own decisions, rather than compare total scores.

1. **A clinic.** Verify the site with an HTML tag, or with a DNS record if you want a Domain property. Google says a site with fewer than 500 pages probably does not need the Page indexing report, so a clinic site of a few dozen pages can live in the Performance and Generative AI reports. Then run the free check. In our study, 44% of dental sites had no short direct answer at the top of any checked page, which is the paragraph that gets quoted, and not something any of the Search Console reports above is built to flag. Watch writes that paragraph for you.

2. **An agency with ten clients.** In Search Console each client gives the agency access to their own property, and the API allows 1,200 queries a minute per site and per user for anyone building reports. There is no Google-made MCP server; community ones wrap the API. On the OperStack side, the free MCP server compares two to four sites on one 100-point scale, which makes a quick pitch slide, and each client on Watch gets their own weekly letter with rivals named. Our pricing page lists one Watch plan and no agency tier; the $149 audit works as a paid first deliverable.

3. **A SaaS company.** The Performance report's branded and non-branded filter shows whether people search for the product by name, and the Generative AI report shows which docs or blog pages appear in AI Overviews. Software buyers, though, ask ChatGPT which tool to use. Our [ContextBolt comparison](/compare/operstack-vs-contextbolt/) explains the distinction between a requested lookup and recurring monitoring. Watch tracks those names every week.

4. **An online shop.** Here Search Console carries most of the weight. Google says sites under 500 pages probably do not need the Page indexing report; a shop with a real catalogue is past that line, the report's example lists stop at 1,000 URLs, and the URL Inspection API allows 2,000 checks a day. OperStack reads ten pages, a hundred in the audit, so it samples your templates rather than your catalogue. Where a shop sells through advice, Watch shows whether ChatGPT names it when a buyer asks where to buy.

## How do you use Search Console and OperStack together?

Use Search Console and Watch as 2 separate records of the same publishing work. Keep the page address, edit date and question set with every observation. Search Console can request a Google recrawl; it cannot order a ChatGPT update, so each system needs its own dated follow-up.

1. **Verify a Domain property with a DNS record.** It is the one method Google accepts for that property type.
2. **Submit your sitemap, and keep it findable.** A sitemap submitted in Search Console is known to Google, but that submission reaches Google alone. Our check looks for a sitemap at the usual paths and in robots.txt, and 42% of the sites in our study had none there, or one that would not open.
3. **Run the OperStack free check.** Note the rivals ChatGPT named and the paragraph the check wrote.
4. **Publish the fix, then request indexing** of that page with URL Inspection.
5. **Watch two signals on the same change.** Over the next weeks, that page's impressions in the Generative AI report show Google's side; the named-or-not line in your Watch letter shows ChatGPT's.
6. **Once a month, compare wording.** Set the real queries from the Performance report beside the ten questions in your Watch letter. Where buyers use words in Google that your main page never uses, the page is missing an answer both systems could quote.

If you work in an AI app, both tools can live in one setup: a community server for Search Console data, and OperStack's free MCP server, with [setup notes](/mcp/) for Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, VS Code, Windsurf and Zed.

## What our data says about discoverable sitemaps

In our 496-site study, the checker could not find or open a sitemap at its supported discovery locations for 208 sites, about 42%. That is a discovery finding from the September 2026 run. It does not establish that those websites were absent from Google's index or had never submitted a sitemap.

The source is the OperStack free check study dated 25 September 2026, covering small-business domains in the US, UK, Australia and Canada. A sitemap can exist at a custom address without being found by a particular checker, which is why the wording of the result matters.

| Sitemap observation | Sites | Meaning of the number |
|---|---|---|
| Not found or unreadable by the checker | 208 | Inspect paths, response and robots declaration |
| Other scored sites | 288, calculated | Not evidence that all sitemap URLs are indexed |
| Total scored domains | 496 | Denominator for this collection run |

A useful operator check starts with the submitted URL in Search Console. Open that exact address in a signed-out browser, confirm it serves an XML sitemap or index, and check whether robots.txt declares it. Then inspect whether the current important pages are actually listed. Each step answers a different question.

Submitting the file to Google does not change its public discoverability for other systems. Conversely, a discoverable sitemap does not force Google to index every URL it contains. Preserve both observations rather than trying to turn them into one success label.

That distinction also helps when reviewing Watch. The email may identify a map or page issue worth fixing, but Search Console remains the place to inspect Google's processing status. The [free OperStack check](/ai-visibility/) supplies a second viewpoint without requiring access to your Google account.
